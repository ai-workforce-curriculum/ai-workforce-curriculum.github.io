Your relationship to the model

To you the model is a colleague with uneven skills: fast, tireless, often right, sometimes confidently wrong. You don't build or run it. You decide what to hand it, how to brief it, how to verify what comes back, and which parts of the job stay yours. Your craft is the judgment around the model, not the model itself.

This org covers AI in everyday professional work: working alongside the models. It's for engineers, analysts, product managers, and managers whose job isn't building AI but whose work now runs through it: delegating to coding agents and assistants, briefing them well, verifying what comes back, and building team habits that make AI-assisted work fast and safe.

What you own

  • Which tasks to delegate to models and agents, and which to keep
  • Briefing: the context, constraints, and examples that get usable output
  • Verification: reviewing, testing, and fact-checking what the model produces
  • Workflows and team habits that make AI-assisted work repeatable and safe
  • Accountability for the result, whoever (or whatever) drafted it

What you don't own: The model, the tools built on it, or the policy that governs them. You're their most demanding user: you report where they fail real work and push for the fixes.

Who you work with

No curriculum owns the model end to end. These are the handoffs with each of the others.

  • AI Startup

    Turn it into a company

    You hand them
    Evidence of where AI changes the economics of a job, which is where the product and hiring bets are
    They hand you
    Decisions about which AI tools the company adopts and how roles change, plus the training and time to adapt

  • AI Governance

    Govern, secure, and assure AI

    You hand them
    How AI is actually being used on the ground, including the shadow uses policy hasn't caught up with
    They hand you
    Clear rules on what may be shared with which tools, and which uses need review, written so people can follow them

  • AI Engineering

    Build with the models

    You hand them
    Workflow requirements and failure reports from daily use, which tell builders what to fix and what to build next
    They hand you
    Tools, agents, and integrations that fit how people actually work, with sensible defaults and guardrails

  • ML Engineering

    Build and train the models

    You hand them
    Concrete examples of where model quality falls short on real tasks, the raw material for better evals and training data
    They hand you
    Models that are better at real work, plus honest documentation of what they're bad at

  • AI Infrastructure

    Run the platforms models live on

    You hand them
    Real usage patterns and pain points (latency, limits, outages) from people whose day depends on the tools
    They hand you
    Reliable, fast, well-documented AI tools with clear limits, so a working day doesn't stall on an outage or a rate limit

How the tracks work

Learn

Each track is a sequence of modules: lecture chapters, hands-on exercises, quizzes, and projects. Start with Module 00 and work forward.

Check your work

The paired solutions repo has a reference walkthrough for every exercise. Attempt each one first; the gap between your answer and the reference is where the learning happens.

Built in the open

The content is written with AI assistance and under ongoing human review, so it can contain mistakes. Report anything wrong through the repo's Issues; fixes ship continuously.
